The road between Ruvu and Mlandizi runs on an embankment across low ground beside the Ruvu River. Every rainy season the same defects come back: potholes, alligator cracks, heaves and ruts. They return even after frequent maintenance, which is the clearest sign that the cause sits below the surface.

## Water from below, not only from above

Most people blame the rain that falls on the road. On this stretch, the bigger problem is the water that comes from underneath. During the rainy season, rainwater and floods from the Ruvu River raise the water table, and that water infiltrates the embankment that carries the pavement.

> This scenario has led to severe defects on the road pavement, such as potholes, alligator cracks, heaves and ruts, regardless of frequent maintenance conducted. From the paper

## What waterlogging does to a pavement

A road pavement works because each layer below it is strong enough to spread the weight of traffic. Soil and gravel are at their strongest when they are dry and well compacted. When they stay wet, they soften and lose much of their bearing capacity, so the layers above have to bend more under every passing truck.

- **Potholes** form where water reaches the base through cracks and traffic washes the softened material out.
- **Alligator cracks** appear as the weakened layers flex under repeated loads until the surface breaks into small blocks.
- **Heaves and ruts** follow as saturated soils swell and are pushed sideways by wheel loads.

## Why patching alone does not last

Patching a pothole replaces the surface, but it leaves the wet embankment underneath. The next heavy rains raise the water table again, the base softens again, and the defect comes back, often next to the old patch. Maintenance money is spent every year on the symptom, while the cause stays in place.

## What our paper sets out

Our paper, *Effects of Waterlogging on the Performance of Ruvu to Mlandizi Road Pavement*, studies this problem and sets out a methodological approach for assessing waterlogging on the embankment and the measures to deal with it. The full method and results are in the paper, which is free to download.

## What it means for anyone building a road

The lesson reaches beyond one road. Wherever a road crosses low ground near a river or a swamp, the water table belongs in the design from the first day, not only the rainfall.

- Investigate the ground and record how high the water rises in the wet season.
- Test the soils soaked as well as dry, because a soaked California bearing ratio is the one that matters on wet ground.
- Design drainage and embankment height for the wet season, not the day of the survey.
